Study Notes on Galatians

What is an Apostle?

From the very beginning of his letter to the Galatians, Paul defends his apostleship. In Galatians 1:1, he identifies himself as an apostle whose commission comes neither from men nor through a man, but through Jesus Christ and God the Father. So, what is an apostle?

Ancient reports about the Ebionites

Irenaeus, Against Heresies 1.26.2

Irenaeus describes the Ebionites’ rejection of Paul:

“They use the Gospel according to Matthew only, and repudiate the Apostle Paul, maintaining that he was an apostate from the law.”

[Irenaeus, Against Heresies 1.26.2; translated by Alexander Roberts and William Rambaut, Ante-Nicene Fathers, vol. 1 (1885)]

Irenaeus, Against Heresies 3.15.1

This passage addresses rejection of Paul’s apostleship; the paragraph itself does not name the Ebionites.

“But again, we allege the same against those who do not recognise Paul as an apostle”

[Irenaeus, Against Heresies 3.15.1; translated by Alexander Roberts and William Rambaut, Ante-Nicene Fathers, vol. 1 (1885); excerpt from the opening sentence]

Epiphanius of Salamis, Panarion 30.16.8–9

Epiphanius attributes to the Ebionites a story that Paul had Greek parents and accepted circumcision to marry a high priest’s daughter. He reports their claim that rejection of the marriage led Paul to oppose Jewish observances, and he rejects the story as fabricated.

“wrote against circumcision, and against the Sabbath and the legislation.”

[Epiphanius, Panarion 30.16.8–9; translated by Frank Williams, Book I, 2nd ed. (2009), p. 144; brief excerpt from 30.16.9]
