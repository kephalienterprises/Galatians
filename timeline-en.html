Study Notes on Galatians

Paul’s Missionary Journeys: A Detailed Timeline

How to read this timeline

Each box separates a place or a connected group of places that Acts or Paul’s letters allow us to place in sequence. Dates marked high confidence are anchored by explicit time statements or external evidence; medium confidence dates are reconstructions from the broader chronology; low confidence dates or identifications remain debated.

The dates are approximate. The traditional three missionary journeys are shown separately from Paul’s later voyage to Rome as a prisoner.

Chronology anchor: The broad sequence follows Acts 9–28 and Galatians 1–2. A representative scholarly chronology places Paul’s conversion around AD 32/33, the Jerusalem Council in AD 48/49, Corinth in AD 50–52, the arrest in AD 57, and Rome in AD 59–60. See the Cambridge Companion chronology.

c. AD 32/33 medium confidence

Damascus: conversion and call

Saul encountered the risen Christ on the road to Damascus and began proclaiming Jesus in the synagogues. The place is explicit; the year is reconstructed from the later intervals in Galatians.

Place: Damascus

References: Acts 9:1–22; Galatians 1:15–16; 1 Corinthians 9:1; 15:8–9

Soon after conversion; duration unknown high sequence / low date

Arabia

Paul says that he went away to Arabia and then returned to Damascus. Neither the location within Arabia nor the length of the stay is given, so this box records only the relative order.

Place: Arabia, then Damascus

References: Galatians 1:16–17

Before the first Jerusalem visit high sequence

Damascus: early preaching

After Arabia, Paul returned to Damascus. His preaching provoked opposition, and disciples eventually lowered him through an opening in the city wall.

Place: Damascus

References: Galatians 1:17; Acts 9:23–25; 2 Corinthians 11:32–33

c. AD 35/36; three years after conversion medium confidence

Jerusalem: first visit after conversion

Paul went to Jerusalem to visit Cephas and stayed fifteen days. He also met James. This short visit is one of the firmest relative markers in Paul’s own account.

Place: Jerusalem

References: Galatians 1:18–20; Acts 9:26–30

c. AD 36–46 medium confidence

Syria and Cilicia

Paul went into the regions of Syria and Cilicia. The letters do not provide a stop-by-stop itinerary for these years, and Acts later places Paul in Tarsus before Barnabas brought him to Antioch.

Region / place: Syria, Cilicia, Tarsus

References: Galatians 1:21–24; Acts 9:30; 11:25

c. AD 44–46 medium confidence

Antioch of Syria: teaching and commissioning

Paul taught with Barnabas in Antioch for a year. The church later set apart Barnabas and Saul for the work to which the Spirit had called them, making Antioch the sending base of the first journey.

Place: Antioch of Syria

References: Acts 11:26; 13:1–3

c. AD 46–47 medium confidence

First journey: Cyprus

Paul, Barnabas, and John Mark sailed from Seleucia to Cyprus. They traveled from Salamis to Paphos, where Sergius Paulus believed after the confrontation with Elymas. From this point Luke begins calling Saul “Paul.”

Route: Antioch → Seleucia → Salamis → Paphos

References: Acts 13:1–12

c. AD 46–47 medium confidence

First journey: southern Galatia

After reaching Perga, Paul preached in Pisidian Antioch, Iconium, Lystra, and Derbe. Opposition followed the mission, including Paul’s stoning at Lystra, but disciples were gathered in each place.

Route: Perga → Pisidian Antioch → Iconium → Lystra → Derbe

References: Acts 13:13–14:20; Galatians 4:13–14

c. AD 47–48 high sequence

First journey: return and Antioch

Paul and Barnabas retraced the route through the new churches, appointed elders, passed through Pisidia and Pamphylia, and sailed back to Antioch to report what God had done among the Gentiles.

Route: Derbe → Lystra → Iconium → Pisidian Antioch → Perga → Attalia → Antioch

References: Acts 14:21–28

c. AD 48/49 medium confidence

Jerusalem: the apostolic council

Paul and Barnabas went from Antioch to Jerusalem over the question of circumcision. The council affirmed the inclusion of Gentile believers without requiring them to become Jews.

Place: Jerusalem

References: Acts 15:1–35; Galatians 2:1–10

c. AD 49–50 high sequence

Second journey: Syria, Cilicia, and Galatia

After separating from Barnabas, Paul and Silas traveled through Syria and Cilicia, strengthening the churches. They revisited Derbe and Lystra, where Timothy joined them, and moved through the Phrygian and Galatian region.

Route: Antioch → Syria and Cilicia → Derbe → Lystra → Phrygia and Galatia

References: Acts 15:36–16:6

c. AD 50 medium confidence

Second journey: Troas and Macedonia

At Troas, Paul received the vision of a Macedonian man. The team crossed to Europe and preached in Philippi, where Lydia believed and Paul and Silas were imprisoned before the church sent them onward.

Route: Troas → Samothrace → Neapolis → Philippi → Amphipolis → Apollonia → Thessalonica → Berea

References: Acts 16:7–17:14

c. AD 50–52; eighteen months in Corinth high confidence

Second journey: Athens and Corinth

Paul preached in Athens and then settled in Corinth, working with Aquila and Priscilla. He remained there eighteen months. The appearance before Gallio provides the key external anchor for the chronology, generally placed around AD 51/52.

Route: Athens → Corinth

References: Acts 17:15–18:17; 18:11; 1 Thessalonians 1:1; 2 Thessalonians 1:1

c. AD 52 medium confidence

Second journey: return through Ephesus and Antioch

Paul sailed to Ephesus, left Priscilla and Aquila there, stopped at Caesarea, went up to Jerusalem, and then returned to Antioch. Acts presents this as the close of the second journey.

Route: Corinth → Cenchreae → Ephesus → Caesarea → Jerusalem → Antioch

References: Acts 18:18–22

c. AD 53 medium confidence

Third journey: Galatia and Phrygia

Paul traveled through the Galatian and Phrygian regions, strengthening the disciples. Acts gives the route and sequence but no individual dates for these visits.

Route: Antioch → Galatia and Phrygia

References: Acts 18:23

c. AD 53–55; about three years high duration

Third journey: Ephesus

Paul taught first in the synagogue and then in the hall of Tyrannus. The message spread through the province of Asia, and Acts records an extended ministry of about three years, including a period of roughly two years in the hall.

Place: Ephesus

References: Acts 19:1–20:1, 31; 1 Corinthians 16:8–9

c. AD 55–57 medium confidence

Third journey: Macedonia, Greece, and the Aegean coast

After the Ephesian upheaval, Paul encouraged the disciples through Macedonia and went to Greece. He spent three months there, then returned through Macedonia, Troas, Assos, Mitylene, Chios, Samos, and Miletus.

Route: Ephesus → Macedonia → Greece → Philippi → Troas → Assos → Mitylene → Chios → Samos → Miletus

References: Acts 20:1–16; Romans 15:25–28; 1 Corinthians 16:5–8

c. AD 57 medium confidence

Jerusalem: arrest at the end of the third journey

Paul traveled via Cos, Rhodes, Patara, Tyre, Ptolemais, and Caesarea before arriving in Jerusalem. He was arrested after the temple dispute and began the legal sequence that would take him to Rome.

Route: Miletus → Cos → Rhodes → Patara → Tyre → Ptolemais → Caesarea → Jerusalem

References: Acts 20:17–21:36

c. AD 57–59; two years high duration

Caesarea: imprisonment and hearings

Paul was held in Caesarea under Felix and then Festus. Acts explicitly says that two years passed before Festus succeeded Felix. Paul appealed to Caesar and was sent under guard.

Place: Caesarea Maritima

References: Acts 23:23–26:32; 24:27; 25:10–12

c. AD 59–60; two years in Rome medium confidence

Rome: voyage and house arrest

Paul sailed from Caesarea, survived a storm and shipwreck at Malta, and reached Rome through Syracuse, Rhegium, and Puteoli. Acts ends with Paul under house arrest for two years, welcoming visitors and proclaiming the kingdom.

Route: Caesarea → Sidon → Myra → Fair Havens → Malta → Syracuse → Rhegium → Puteoli → Rome

References: Acts 27:1–28:31
