Study Notes on Galatians

Paul’s Missionary Journeys

Overview

This timeline places Paul’s missionary activity in the broad chronology of Acts. The dates are approximate, since the chronology of Paul’s life and the relationship between Acts and Galatians remain debated.

The traditional three missionary journeys are shown separately from Paul’s later voyage to Rome as a prisoner.

c. AD 33–46 — Preparation

Conversion, Arabia, and Antioch

After encountering Christ, Paul spent time in Arabia, returned to Damascus, visited Jerusalem, and later worked from Antioch with Barnabas. These years formed the setting for his call to Gentile mission.

Places: Damascus, Arabia, Jerusalem, Tarsus, Antioch of Syria

References: Galatians 1:15–24; Acts 9:1–30; 11:25–26; 13:1–3

c. AD 46–48 — First journey

Cyprus and southern Galatia

Sent out by the church at Antioch, Paul traveled with Barnabas and John Mark. After preaching across Cyprus, they crossed to Asia Minor, where Paul proclaimed the gospel in Pisidian Antioch, Iconium, Lystra, and Derbe before retracing the route home.

Route: Antioch → Seleucia → Cyprus → Perga → Pisidian Antioch → Iconium → Lystra → Derbe → Antioch

References: Acts 13:1–14:28; Galatians 4:13–14

c. AD 49–50 — Council and transition

Jerusalem and Antioch

The question of Gentile circumcision brought Paul and Barnabas to Jerusalem. The apostles and elders affirmed that Gentile believers were not required to become Jews, after which Paul began a new journey with Silas.

Places: Antioch of Syria, Jerusalem

References: Acts 15:1–35; Galatians 2:1–10

c. AD 49–52 — Second journey

From Galatia into Macedonia and Greece

Paul and Silas revisited the churches in Syria, Cilicia, and Galatia, adding Timothy to the team. A vision redirected them to Macedonia, leading to new churches in Philippi, Thessalonica, and Berea, followed by ministry in Athens and an extended stay in Corinth.

Route: Antioch → Derbe → Lystra → Phrygia and Galatia → Troas → Philippi → Thessalonica → Berea → Athens → Corinth → Ephesus → Caesarea → Antioch

References: Acts 15:36–18:22; Galatians 1:6–9; 3:1–5; 4:13

c. AD 53–57 — Third journey

Galatia, Ephesus, Macedonia, and Jerusalem

Paul strengthened the churches in Galatia and Phrygia, then spent about three years in Ephesus. From there he traveled through Macedonia and Greece, encouraging the churches before returning to Jerusalem with the collection for believers there.

Route: Antioch → Galatia and Phrygia → Ephesus → Macedonia → Greece → Troas → Miletus → Jerusalem

References: Acts 18:23–21:17; Romans 15:25–28; 1 Corinthians 16:1–4

c. AD 57–60 — Later voyage

Jerusalem to Rome as a prisoner

After his arrest in Jerusalem, Paul was transferred to Caesarea and eventually sent to Rome. This final journey is not one of the traditional missionary journeys, but it carried his witness to the heart of the Roman world.

Route: Jerusalem → Caesarea → Sidon → Crete → Malta → Syracuse → Rhegium → Puteoli → Rome

References: Acts 21:27–28:31
